I'll start by saying *I probably have too many basses*.

I don't really *collect* basses, in the sense that many collectors accumulate instruments for value, appreciation.
not really to *play* them.

Every bass I have has a story, a reason I bought it.  Some I picked up new, some used.
I consider all of them *working* basses - they are meant to be played, not sit in a case, or hanging on a wall.

## What I don't have

So far I'm missing uprights and short-scales.
I have an itch for both, but not quite ready for either.

I don't have vintage instruments either, other than a beat up '78 P/J quietly waiting for a restore.

## Categories

Before jumping into the inventory,
it is important to understand the categories and characteristcs my basses fall into:

### Gigging vs Recording

I have basses I like to perform with, vs. what I might record (or loan to someone to record).

The needs can be different:

- Performances favor one bass (perhaps with a backup) that can suitably cover all the songs I might play
- Recording can be highly specific (if looking for a specific sound)

My Fenders arrived to my collection mostly to support my home studio recording options for others - I was working towards sound engineering and music production versus being a serious bass player.  There is a long history for that, but not subject for discussion now.

The Precision and Jazz Basses are mostly passive, and give an iconic sound.
Music production favors them because it is typically easy to get them to sit in a mix,
while other basses might require some hunting to get classic sounds out of them.

Similarly, I chose to pick up Sterlings for similar reasons - they have iconic late 70's and early 80's bass tones.

Getting back into playing, I noticed a more natural gravitation to Ibanez - I had a history with them back in the early 90's,
and really like the Sound Gear lines for fast necks.

This is not to say I could not gig with the Fenders/Sterlinggs, but there are other considerations

### Reliability/Durability/Maintenance

What I look for here is consistency:

- How often do I need to tweak my setup?
- How consistent is the intonation?
- If any part of the setup is active, how often do I need to swap batteries, is it predictable?  Or can I goto passive mode on the fly if necessary?
- Minimal noise
- Pots, electrics are mostly carefree

Every bass in my collection is suitable for recording or gigs, although I notice Fenders tend to be inconsistent out-of-box (may require pro setup, even for mid-tier).

Ibanez and Sterlings have been very consistent out-of-box, and easy to tweak the setup.

I've had some inconsistent battery drain, and, I'm getting intermittent pickup noise out of my Ibanez SRMS725.  It hosts the Fishman Fluence active setup that has no passive backup mode - I got this one used, and it has gotten some tough love during a couple of gigs, so I'm not ready to critize Ibanez.

I have the same setup on my Ibanez EHBMS Fishman Fluent bass, and no problems with it.

All that said, I am shying away from active-only basses for gigs - I've been burned a couple of times with what I would have qualified as a bad batch of batteries.
